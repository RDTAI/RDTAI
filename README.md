## About me

I am a PhD student in the **Department of Industrial and Systems Engineering (ISE)** at **The Hong Kong Polytechnic University**, working with the **AI Reliability Lab**.

My research focuses on **reliable machine learning**. I investigate failure mechanisms during model training and inference, and develop statistically grounded methods to make deep learning predictions more dependable.

My current work focuses on **conformal prediction**, conventional classification, and medical image classification. I am especially interested in understanding not only whether a method works, but also **when its reliability guarantees break and how those failures can be detected**.

## Research focus

- **Conformal prediction** — finite-sample uncertainty quantification and informative prediction sets
- **Failure analysis** — diagnosing model failures during training and inference
- **Distribution shift** — reliability under changing populations, domains, and acquisition settings
- **Medical image classification** — clinically meaningful uncertainty and subgroup-aware evaluation
- **Calibration and selective prediction** — knowing when a model should abstain

## Questions I care about

- When and why do deep learning models become unreliable?
- How can we detect reliability failures before they cause harm?
- When does conformal coverage degrade under distribution shift?
- How should uncertainty be evaluated across classes and clinically relevant subgroups?
- Can statistical guarantees be made useful in real-world medical decision pipelines?
